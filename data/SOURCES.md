# Sources behind the exercise mappings

Fiber's mappings describe **emphasis, never isolation**. They're sensible defaults based on EMG studies,
a few hypertrophy (muscle growth) studies, and common coaching consensus. They aren't medical fact.

Keep in mind:
- **EMG** measures how active a muscle is during a lift. That isn't the same as how much it grows, and
  surface EMG can't separate the regions of one muscle very well.
- **Hypertrophy studies** measure real growth, but they're usually small (10–40 people) and short (5–12 weeks).

Where a split rests mostly on coaching convention, the exercise has a `note` and it's listed under
"Judgment calls" below.

---

## Chest (upper / mid / lower)
- Trebs, Brandenburg & Pitney (2010). *An electromyography analysis of 3 muscles surrounding the shoulder
  joint during the performance of a chest press exercise at several angles.* J Strength Cond Res 24(7).
  https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4053707?lng=en
  - The upper (clavicular) chest was more active at steeper inclines; the mid/lower (sternocostal) part was more active when flat.
- Rodríguez-Ridao et al. (2020). *Effect of five bench inclinations on the electromyographic activity of
  the pectoralis major, anterior deltoid, and triceps brachii during the bench press exercise.* IJERPH.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33049982/
  - Supports the moderate incline (about 30 degrees) used for the upper-chest presses.

## Shoulders (front / side / rear delts)
- Coratella et al. (2020). *An electromyographic analysis of lateral raise variations and frontal raise
  in competitive bodybuilders.* IJERPH 17(17):6015. https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/17/17/6015
  - Lateral raises put the main emphasis on the side delts, and front raises on the front delts. Upper traps help.

## Traps (upper / mid-lower)
- Ekstrom et al. (2003). *Surface electromyographic analysis of exercises for the trapezius and serratus
  anterior muscles.* J Orthop Sports Phys Ther 33(5). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12774999/
  - Shrugs gave the highest upper-trap activity. Horizontal pulling with the arms out wide (rear-delt and row
    style moves) gave the highest mid-trap activity.

## Lats (upper / lower)
- Lusk, Hale & Russell (2010). *Grip width and forearm orientation effects on muscle activity during the
  lat pull-down.* J Strength Cond Res.
  https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/grip-width-and-forearm-orientation-effects-on-muscle-activity-dur/
  - Grip width did **not** clearly change overall lat activity. This is why the upper/lower lat split is
    labelled weak evidence.

## Lower back (erectors)
- McAllister et al. (2014). *Muscle activation during various hamstring exercises.* J Strength Cond Res.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24149748/
  - Recorded erector spinae alongside the hamstrings in the Romanian deadlift and good morning.
- Back extensions and deadlifts as the main lower-back exercises are based on coaching consensus.

## Biceps (long / short head)
- Oliveira et al. (2009). *Effect of the shoulder position on the biceps brachii EMG in different dumbbell
  curls.* J Sports Sci Med 8(1). https://jssm.org/jssm-08-24.xml-abst
  - Incline curls start with the long head stretched. Preacher curls start with it shortened. The study
    measured only the long head, so it doesn't settle the long vs short head question.
- Pedrosa et al. (2023). *Training in the Initial Range of Motion Promotes Greater Muscle Adaptations
  Than at Final in the Arm Curl.* Sports. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9960616
  - Covers growth along the muscle, not the individual heads.

## Triceps (long / lateral + medial)
- Maeo et al. (2023). *Triceps brachii hypertrophy is substantially greater after elbow extension training
  performed in the overhead versus neutral arm position.* Eur J Sport Sci 23(7).
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/
  - Overhead extensions grew the triceps more than pushdowns did, especially the long head. This is the
    basis for mapping overhead work to `triceps_long` and pushdowns to `triceps_lateral`.

## Forearms
- No specific study. Wrist curls, reverse curls and hammer curls are mapped from coaching consensus and basic anatomy.

## Abs (upper / lower) and obliques
- Clark et al. (2003). *Electromyographic comparison of the upper and lower rectus abdominis during
  abdominal exercises.* J Strength Cond Res. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12930172/
  - No significant difference between upper and lower rectus abdominis in any exercise tested.
- Lehman & McGill (2001). *Quantification of the differences in electromyographic activity magnitude
  between the upper and lower portions of the rectus abdominis muscle during selected trunk exercises.*
  Phys Ther 81(5). https://doi.org/10.1093/ptj/81.5.1096
  - Often cited for the view that upper and lower abs act largely together.
- Escamilla et al. (2006). *An electromyographic analysis of commercial and common abdominal exercises.*
  J Orthop Sports Phys Ther 36(2). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16494072/
  - Recorded the upper and lower rectus abdominis and the obliques across common ab exercises.
- McGill, McDermott & Fenwick (2009). *Comparison of different strongman events: trunk muscle activation
  and lumbar spine motion, load, and stiffness.* J Strength Cond Res. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19528856/
  - The suitcase carry relies heavily on the side of the trunk (obliques, quadratus lumborum). Kept for
    when carries are added (see Judgment calls).

## Quads (rectus femoris / vasti)
- Ema et al. (2013). *Inhomogeneous architectural changes of the quadriceps femoris induced by resistance
  training.* Eur J Appl Physiol. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23949789/
  - With knee-extension training, the rectus femoris grew more than the vasti.
- Zabaleta-Korta et al. (2021). *The role of exercise selection in regional muscle hypertrophy: a randomized
  controlled trial.* J Sports Sci. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34743671/
  - Leg extensions grew the rectus femoris. Smith-machine squats grew the vastus lateralis.
- Kubo, Ikebukuro & Yata (2019). *Effects of squat training with different depths on lower limb muscle
  volumes.* Eur J Appl Physiol. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y
  - Squats grew the vasti and adductors but not the rectus femoris. Deep squats grew the glutes and adductors more.

## Adductors
- Kubo et al. (2019), see Quads above: deep squats grow the adductors.
- Serner et al. (2014). *EMG evaluation of hip adduction exercises for soccer players.* Br J Sports Med 48(14).
  https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/48/14/1108
  - Adductor activity across hip adduction exercises.

## Glutes (max) and glute med
- Contreras et al. (2015). *A comparison of gluteus maximus, biceps femoris, and vastus lateralis
  electromyographic activity in the back squat and barbell hip thrust exercises.* J Appl Biomech.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26214739/
  - The hip thrust gave more glute-max activity than the back squat.
- DiStefano et al. (2009). *Gluteal muscle activation during common therapeutic exercises.* J Orthop Sports
  Phys Ther 39(7). https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2009.2796
  - Hip abduction gave the highest glute-med activity.

## Hamstrings
- Maeo et al. (2021). *Greater hamstrings muscle hypertrophy but similar damage protection after training
  at long versus short muscle lengths.* Med Sci Sports Exerc 53(4). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/
  - Seated leg curls grew the hamstrings more than lying leg curls did. This is why the seated curl cue asks for a forward lean.
- McAllister et al. (2014), see Lower back above: RDLs and leg curls both work the hamstrings strongly.

## Calves
- Kinoshita et al. (2023). *Triceps surae muscle hypertrophy is greater after standing versus seated
  calf-raise training.* Front Physiol 14. https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1272106/full
  - Standing raises grew the gastrocnemius much more than seated raises. Seated raises still train the soleus.
    Both map to the single `calves` region.

## Judgment calls (weak evidence or format limits)

**Weak regional evidence. These exercises have a `note`:**
- **Upper vs lower lats.** Wide-grip pulldowns and pull-ups map to `lats_upper`. Single-arm rows toward the
  hip, close-grip pulldowns and chin-ups map to `lats_lower`. EMG doesn't clearly support a split.
  Exercises in one lat region don't give secondary credit to the other, so the split stays visible.
- **Barbell row and seated cable row.** Both list `traps_mid` and `lats_lower` as primary.
- **Straight-arm pulldown.** Lists both lat regions as primary.
- **Upper vs lower abs.** Crunches and cable crunches map to `abs_upper`. Leg raises and reverse crunches map
  to `abs_lower`. EMG studies mostly show no clear difference.
- **Biceps long vs short head.** Arm-behind-body curls (incline, Bayesian) map to `biceps_long`.
  Arm-in-front curls (preacher, spider) map to `biceps_short`. The barbell curl gets both heads.
- **Hammer curl.** Mapped to `biceps_long` + `forearms`. It mainly works the brachialis and brachioradialis,
  and the brachialis isn't one of Fiber's regions.

**Mapping choices:**
- **Leg extension and sissy squat.** Primary is `quads_rectus`, with the vasti as secondary. In reality the
  vasti work hard here too. Making them secondary keeps the rectus/vasti split meaningful.
- **Squat-pattern lifts.** Primary is `quads_vasti` (plus `glutes` for the back squat). The rectus femoris
  gets no credit, in line with Kubo 2019 and Zabaleta-Korta 2021.
- **Cossack squat.** Mapped to `adductors` from coaching consensus; I found no specific study.
- **Back extension and deadlift.** These are the only two `lower_back` primaries, which is the minimum.
- **Lower chest.** Decline press, dips and high-to-low flies map to `chest_lower`. This rests mostly on
  coaching convention plus the EMG finding that the sternocostal head is favoured at flatter or downward
  angles.

**Pattern choices:**
- Machine hip adduction uses `hip_adduction`, the shrugs use `shrug`, and the wrist curl uses `wrist`.
  These three patterns were added to the allowed list for exactly these moves.
- Dips use `vertical_push`, because the body moves vertically.
- **Carries are deferred to a later version.** Farmer's and suitcase carries are measured in distance or time,
  not reps, so they don't fit double progression. They'll come back once the data model has distance/time
  units. The `carry` pattern stays in the allowed list for that.

**Equipment limits (fixed equipment list):**
- The 45-degree back extension uses `machines`, because there's no hyperextension bench id.
- The skull crusher uses `barbell`, because there's no EZ-bar id.
- The Nordic curl uses `[]`, but it needs something to anchor the heels. Its cue says so (a pad or a partner).
- The cable stations for pulldowns and rows use `cables`.
- Squats, presses and racks: there's no rack id, so the barbell lifts assume the barbell comes with a rack.
